## The Short Answer

Bid'a (بدعة) literally means "innovation." The vast majority of classical scholars — including [Imam al-Nawawi](/scholars/imam-al-nawawi), [al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam](/scholars/al-izz-ibn-abd-al-salam), [Imam al-Shafi'i](/scholars/imam-al-shafii), and [Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani](/scholars/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani) — divide bid'a into categories ranging from obligatory to prohibited. The claim that "every single innovation is forbidden" is a misreading of one hadith, contradicted by how the hadith's own commentators explain it.

## The Hadith and Its Explanation

<HadithBlock
  arabic="كُلُّ بِدْعَةٍ ضَلَالَةٌ"
  translation="Every innovation is misguidance."
  source="Sahih Muslim, no. 867"
  narrator="Jabir ibn Abdullah (رضي الله عنه)"
/>

[Imam al-Nawawi](/scholars/imam-al-nawawi) — the primary commentator on Sahih Muslim — explains directly:

<ScholarQuote
  scholar="Imam al-Nawawi"
  scholarSlug="imam-al-nawawi"
  title="Greatest commentator on Sahih Muslim"
  dates="d. 676 AH / 1278 CE"
  source="Sharh Sahih Muslim, commentary on this hadith"
  quote="This is a general statement that is particularized. What is meant is the majority of innovations. Scholars say it is five types: obligatory, recommended, permissible, disliked, and prohibited."
/>

## The Companion Proof

<HadithBlock
  arabic="نِعْمَتِ الْبِدْعَةُ هَذِهِ"
  translation="What an excellent bid'a this is!"
  source="Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 2010"
  narrator="Umar ibn al-Khattab (رضي الله عنه), referring to congregational tarawih"
/>

If "every bid'a" were categorically forbidden, [Umar](/scholars/umar-ibn-al-khattab) — the second Caliph — would be committing a sin and misleading the entire community. The only coherent reading: the hadith is a general statement with exceptions.

## The Prophetic Encouragement

<HadithBlock
  arabic="مَنْ سَنَّ فِي الْإِسْلَامِ سُنَّةً حَسَنَةً فَلَهُ أَجْرُهَا وَأَجْرُ مَنْ عَمِلَ بِهَا بَعْدَهُ"
  translation="Whoever introduces a good practice in Islam will have its reward and the reward of those who act upon it after him."
  source="Sahih Muslim, no. 1017"
  narrator="Jarir ibn Abdullah (رضي الله عنه)"
/>

<MisconceptionBlock
  claim="The hadith says EVERY bid'a is misguidance — no exceptions."
  response={
    <>
      The scholars who narrated, authenticated, and commented on this hadith — <a href="/scholars/imam-al-nawawi">al-Nawawi</a>, <a href="/scholars/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani">Ibn Hajar</a>, <a href="/scholars/al-izz-ibn-abd-al-salam">al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam</a> — all state that &quot;every&quot; here is qualified by other evidence. The Quran itself uses &quot;every&quot; non-absolutely dozens of times. Rejecting their explanation while relying on the hadith they transmitted is a contradiction.
    </>
  }
/>

<MisconceptionBlock
  claim="If the Prophet ﷺ didn't do it, it can't be part of Islam."
  response={
    <>
      The Prophet ﷺ did not compile the Quran into a book, build a minaret, add vowel marks to the Quran, or organize tarawih into a congregation. The Companions and later scholars did these things, and they are considered among the greatest services to Islam. The absence of the Prophet ﷺ doing something does not constitute its prohibition — only an explicit prohibition does.
    </>
  }
/>
