## The Short Answer

{/* CONTENT PLACEHOLDER — Structural scaffolding for the MDX pipeline. Actual content to be written by a qualified human author. */}

Bid'a (بدعة) literally means "innovation" or "something new." In Islamic scholarship, the vast majority of classical scholars — including Imam al-Nawawi, al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam, and Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani — divide bid'a into **good and bad categories**, following the five rulings of Islamic law (*al-ahkam al-khamsa*).

The common claim that "every single innovation is forbidden" is a misreading of the hadith in Sahih Muslim. The scholars who actually compiled and commented on these hadith collections explain that the general statement is qualified by other evidence.

## Why This Matters

Understanding bid'a properly is essential because the misapplication of this concept is used to:

- Condemn practices accepted by the majority of scholars for centuries
- Create division among Muslims over settled matters
- Undermine the authority of traditional Islamic scholarship
