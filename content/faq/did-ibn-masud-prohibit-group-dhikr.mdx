## The Short Answer

No — not in a way that cancels the explicit Prophetic evidence for gatherings of dhikr. The narration about Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (رضي الله عنه) criticizing people counting dhikr with pebbles is a common polemical proof-text, but SeekersGuidance and traditional scholars raise three points about it.

## The Three Problems with Using This Narration as Proof

**1. Questions about its establishment.** The chain of this narration has been questioned by hadith scholars. Even if accepted, a Companion's personal action cannot override explicit Prophetic hadiths in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim that describe and praise gatherings of dhikr.

**2. It cannot override the Prophet ﷺ.** The Prophet ﷺ said: "No people gather to remember Allah but the angels surround them, mercy covers them, tranquility descends upon them, and Allah mentions them to those who are with Him" (Sahih Muslim, no. 2700). This — and similar rigorously authenticated hadiths — directly and unambiguously describe group dhikr and praise it in the highest terms.

**3. A coherent reading of what Ibn Mas'ud was criticizing.** A plausible interpretation is that Ibn Mas'ud condemned the belief that a *specific counting method* was divinely required — that is, he objected to treating a particular technique as binding sunnah, not to the act of group remembrance itself. This reading is consistent with the broader principle that scholars distinguish between the act (permissible) and claiming a specific modality is obligatory (blameworthy).

## The Principle

SeekersGuidance's consistent position: group dhikr is permitted — and in some contexts praiseworthy — based on multiple authentic hadiths. The key conditions are that it should not be excessively loud or disruptive, and one must not treat a particular organized form as a specific Prophetic sunnah binding on all.
