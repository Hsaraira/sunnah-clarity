## The Short Answer

*Hadra* (حضرة, literally "presence") refers to a gathering of dhikr that may include standing in a circle, singing devotional poetry, rhythmic movement, and sometimes duff accompaniment. Its permissibility depends on its specific elements — not on the label — and classical scholars applied element-by-element fiqh to evaluate it.

## How Scholars Evaluate Hadra

SeekersGuidance applies a straightforward approach: correctly identify the practice's elements, then apply established rulings to each one.

- **Dhikr (remembrance of Allah)** — permitted and praiseworthy by overwhelming evidence
- **Standing** — permitted; there is no prohibition on standing during worship or dhikr
- **Duff accompaniment** — permitted with conditions (see [What is the duff?](/faq/what-is-the-duff))
- **Movement/swaying** — classical Shafi'i authorities permitted movement during gatherings of remembrance and audition, provided it avoids licentiousness
- **Forbidden instruments** — impermissible, and their presence makes attending the gathering impermissible
- **Free-mixing** — impermissible, and its presence makes the gathering impermissible regardless of the dhikr

A contemporary Shafi'i jurist cited by SeekersGuidance allows the duff and permits movement if it avoids indecency — while forbidding attendance at gatherings that include forbidden instruments or impermissible mixing.

## The Salafi/Wahhabi Objection

The common objection is that hadra is "imitation" or "innovation" because it involves forms of movement not found in the first generation's practice of dhikr. SeekersGuidance responds that:

1. The *elements* of hadra — dhikr, standing, poetry, duff — are each individually permitted
2. Combining individually permissible elements does not create a prohibition
3. Where a matter is genuinely differed upon among qualified scholars, sweeping repudiation is not permitted
