## The Short Answer

Yes. Tasawwuf (Sufism) is the traditional Islamic science of spiritual purification — the pursuit of *ihsan* (spiritual excellence) as defined in the Hadith of Jibril (Sahih Muslim). It has been an integral part of Sunni Islam since the earliest generations.

The claim that Sufism is "foreign to Islam" contradicts the explicit practice and writings of the greatest scholars in Islamic history.

## The Key Evidence

The Hadith of Jibril (Sahih Muslim) establishes three dimensions of the religion: Islam (practice), Iman (belief), and Ihsan (spiritual excellence — "to worship Allah as though you see Him"). Tasawwuf is the science that systematizes the third dimension, just as fiqh systematizes the first and aqida systematizes the second.

Imam Malik, the founder of the Maliki school, stated: "Whoever practices tasawwuf without learning fiqh has become a heretic. Whoever learns fiqh without practicing tasawwuf has become corrupt. And whoever combines the two has reached the truth."

## Who Practiced It

The greatest scholars of Sunni Islam practiced and affirmed tasawwuf: Imam al-Ghazali (whose *Ihya Ulum al-Din* is perhaps the most influential book in Islamic history after the Quran), Imam al-Nawawi, Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, Imam al-Suyuti, and countless others. These are not fringe figures — they are the very foundations of Sunni scholarship.

Tasawwuf deals with purifying the heart of diseases like arrogance, envy, showing off, and heedlessness, and cultivating virtues like sincerity, humility, gratitude, and love of Allah. All of these are core Quranic and prophetic concerns.
