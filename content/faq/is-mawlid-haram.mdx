## The Short Answer

No. The vast majority of Sunni scholars throughout history have considered the mawlid to be permissible and praiseworthy. This includes [Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani](/scholars/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani) (the greatest commentator on Sahih al-Bukhari), [Imam al-Suyuti](/scholars/imam-al-suyuti) (who wrote an entire treatise defending it), [Imam al-Nawawi](/scholars/imam-al-nawawi), and scholars from all four madhabs spanning over eight centuries.

## The Prophet ﷺ Honored His Own Birthday

<HadithBlock
  arabic="ذَاكَ يَوْمٌ وُلِدْتُ فِيهِ وَيَوْمٌ بُعِثْتُ أَوْ أُنْزِلَ عَلَيَّ فِيهِ"
  translation="That is the day on which I was born, and the day on which I received revelation."
  source="Sahih Muslim, no. 1162"
  narrator="Abu Qatada al-Ansari (رضي الله عنه), when asked why the Prophet ﷺ fasted on Mondays"
/>

The Prophet ﷺ marked the day of his birth with an act of worship — fasting. He did this every week for his entire life. This establishes the prophetic precedent: the day of his birth is significant, and honoring it through worship is his own practice.

## Ibn Hajar's Legal Reasoning

<ScholarQuote
  scholar="Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani"
  scholarSlug="ibn-hajar-al-asqalani"
  title="Commentator on Sahih al-Bukhari"
  dates="d. 852 AH / 1449 CE"
  source="Fath al-Bari; reported by al-Suyuti in Husn al-Maqsid"
  quote="I have found a sound basis for the mawlid from the Sunna. When the Prophet ﷺ arrived in Medina, he found the Jews fasting on the day of Ashura. They said: 'This is the day on which Allah saved Musa from drowning, so we fast in gratitude.' The Prophet ﷺ said: 'We have more right to Musa than you.' So he fasted on that day. From this we derive that showing gratitude to Allah for a blessing granted on a particular day — whether by fasting, giving charity, or other forms of worship — is sound. And what blessing is greater than the appearance of the Prophet ﷺ, the Prophet of Mercy, on that day?"
/>

This reasoning comes from the greatest hadith scholar of his era. The logic is straightforward: if the Prophet ﷺ approved of fasting in gratitude for a blessing tied to a specific day, then expressing gratitude for the birth of the Prophet ﷺ — the greatest blessing to humanity — is even more warranted.

## The Ashura Hadith That Establishes the Principle

<HadithBlock
  arabic="هَذَا يَوْمٌ عَظِيمٌ أَنْجَى اللهُ فِيهِ مُوسَى وَقَوْمَهُ وَغَرَّقَ فِرْعَوْنَ وَقَوْمَهُ"
  translation="This is a great day on which Allah saved Musa and his people and drowned Pharaoh and his people."
  source="Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 2004; Sahih Muslim, no. 1130"
  narrator="Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنهما)"
/>

The Jews fasted annually on the anniversary of the day Allah saved Musa. The Prophet ﷺ did not say "commemorating an anniversary is bid'a" — he affirmed the practice and participated in it. Ibn Hajar extracts the general principle: *marking anniversaries of divine blessings with worship is Sunna*, not innovation.

## What a Traditional Mawlid Gathering Includes

A traditional mawlid is not a party or celebration in the secular sense. It typically includes:

1. **Recitation of the Quran** — especially Sura Ya Sin and verses describing the Prophet ﷺ
2. **Sending blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ** — fulfilling the Quranic command (33:56)
3. **Studying his biography (sira)** — his birth, his character, his miracles
4. **Poetry in his praise** — a practice established by the Companions (Hassan ibn Thabit composed poetry praised by the Prophet ﷺ himself)
5. **Feeding people** — an act of charity and community building

Every individual element is established in the Quran and Sunna. Combining established acts of worship into a gathering does not transform them into a sin.

## The Quranic Command to Rejoice in Allah's Grace

<QuranVerse
  arabic="قُلْ بِفَضْلِ اللَّهِ وَبِرَحْمَتِهِ فَبِذَٰلِكَ فَلْيَفْرَحُوا هُوَ خَيْرٌ مِمَّا يَجْمَعُونَ"
  translation="Say: In the bounty of Allah and in His mercy — in that let them rejoice. It is better than what they accumulate."
  reference="Quran 10:58"
/>

Classical scholars identify the "mercy" (*rahma*) in this verse as including the Prophet ﷺ himself, based on Quran 21:107: "We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds." If the Prophet ﷺ is the mercy sent to humanity, and Allah commands rejoicing in His mercy, then rejoicing at the arrival of that mercy is Quranically grounded.

## Al-Suyuti's Defense

<ScholarQuote
  scholar="Imam al-Suyuti"
  scholarSlug="imam-al-suyuti"
  title="Mujtahid-level scholar, author of over 500 works"
  dates="d. 911 AH / 1505 CE"
  source="Husn al-Maqsid fi Amal al-Mawlid"
  quote="The basis of the mawlid — gathering people, reciting the Quran, narrating the events of his birth, and setting out food — is a praiseworthy innovation (bid'a hasana) for which its practitioner is rewarded, because of the veneration of the rank of the Prophet ﷺ and the expression of joy at his noble birth."
/>

Al-Suyuti wrote an entire treatise on the mawlid (*Husn al-Maqsid fi Amal al-Mawlid*), assembling evidence from the Quran, Sunna, and classical scholars. He was not a fringe figure — he was one of the most prolific scholars in Islamic history.

## What About Abu Lahab?

A remarkable narration illustrates the reward even a disbeliever received for rejoicing at the Prophet's birth:

<HadithBlock
  translation="Abu Lahab's punishment in the Fire is lightened every Monday because he freed his slave-girl Thuwaybah when she brought him the news of the Prophet's ﷺ birth."
  source="Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 5101 (mentioned by Ibn Hajar in Fath al-Bari)"
  narrator="Reported via 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr"
/>

If a disbeliever — someone condemned in the Quran by name (Sura al-Masad) — receives relief in his punishment for expressing joy at the Prophet's birth, what about a believer who gathers to honor the Prophet ﷺ with Quran, salawat, and charity?
