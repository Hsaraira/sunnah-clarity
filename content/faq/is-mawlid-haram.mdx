## The Short Answer

{/* CONTENT PLACEHOLDER — Structural scaffolding. Actual content to be written by a qualified human author. */}

No. The vast majority of Sunni scholars throughout history — including luminaries like Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, Imam al-Suyuti, and scholars from all four madhabs — have considered the mawlid to be permissible and praiseworthy.

The Prophet ﷺ himself honored the day of his birth by fasting on Mondays, saying: "That is the day on which I was born" (Sahih Muslim).

## The Scholarly Evidence

Imam al-Suyuti wrote an entire treatise (*Husn al-Maqsid fi Amal al-Mawlid*) establishing the permissibility of the mawlid. Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani — the greatest commentator on Sahih al-Bukhari — affirmed its basis in the Sunnah.

The mawlid has been practiced across the Muslim world for over eight centuries. It is not a fringe opinion — it is the mainstream scholarly position.
