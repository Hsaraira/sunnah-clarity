## The Short Answer

No. The vast majority of Sunni scholars throughout history have considered the mawlid to be permissible and praiseworthy. This includes Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani (the greatest commentator on Sahih al-Bukhari), Imam al-Suyuti (who wrote an entire treatise in its defense), and scholars from all four madhabs spanning over eight centuries.

## The Prophetic Basis

The Prophet ﷺ himself marked his day of birth. When asked why he fasted on Mondays, he said: "That is the day on which I was born, and the day on which I received revelation" (Sahih Muslim). He honored his birthday through an act of worship.

Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani derived the legal basis from the hadith about the Prophet ﷺ finding the Jews fasting on the day of Ashura in gratitude for Allah saving Musa. Ibn Hajar reasoned: if fasting in gratitude for a blessing on the anniversary of that blessing is praiseworthy, then expressing gratitude for the birth of the Prophet ﷺ — the greatest blessing to humanity — is even more so.

## What the Mawlid Is

A traditional mawlid gathering includes recitation of the Quran, sending blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ, studying his biography, and expressions of communal joy and gratitude. No scholar claims it is obligatory — the position is that it is recommended or permissible, and those who observe it with good intention are rewarded.
