## The Short Answer

Yes. Visiting graves is not only permissible — the Prophet ﷺ explicitly encouraged it. He said: "I had forbidden you from visiting graves, but now visit them, for they remind you of the Hereafter" (Sahih Muslim, no. 977). He himself visited the graveyard of al-Baqi' regularly and taught specific supplications to recite there.

## The Evidence

The Prophet ﷺ initially prohibited visiting graves because the early Muslims had recently left pre-Islamic practices associated with ancestor worship. Once monotheism was firmly established, he lifted the prohibition and actively encouraged grave visitation as a means of remembering death and the Hereafter.

He also visited his mother's grave and wept at it, as narrated in Sahih Muslim. And Aisha (رضي الله عنها) asked the Prophet ﷺ what to say when visiting the graveyard, and he taught her a specific supplication — proving that grave visitation was a normal part of Muslim life.

## What About Visiting the Graves of Righteous People?

Visiting the graves of the righteous — scholars, saints, and pious Muslims — to make supplication to Allah and seek blessings from the proximity is supported by the practice of the Companions and established scholars. The key principle is that supplication (*du'a*) is directed to Allah alone. Visiting a grave to ask the deceased for things independently of Allah is not permitted — but asking Allah at such places, seeking the barakah of proximity to the righteous, is an established practice.
