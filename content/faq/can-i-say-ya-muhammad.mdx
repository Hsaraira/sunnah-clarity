## The Short Answer

Yes. SeekersGuidance treats saying "Ya Muhammad" as permitted, citing the well-known *Hadith of the Blind Man* as the primary evidence. The phrase appears within a supplication directed to Allah, and it has been part of established devotional usage throughout Muslim history.

## The Evidence

The Prophet ﷺ taught a blind man to make the following du'a:

> "O Allah, I ask You and I turn to You through my Prophet Muhammad, the Prophet of mercy. O Muhammad (*Ya Muhammad*), I turn through you to my Lord regarding this need of mine, that it may be fulfilled."

This hadith is narrated in Sunan al-Tirmidhi and graded *sahih* by al-Tirmidhi and others. The du'a is addressed to Allah — the Prophet ﷺ is invoked as a *means* (wasila), not as an independent source of power.

## The Distinction That Matters

The critical distinction is between:

- **Calling on the Prophet ﷺ as part of a supplication to Allah** — this is tawassul and is permitted
- **Calling on the Prophet ﷺ as though he independently controls the universe** — this would be incompatible with tawhid

The first is what the hadith teaches. The second is what no scholar permits. SeekersGuidance explicitly warns that believers should seek assistance from Allah alone, while permitting tawassul and devotional expressions of love that fall within proper belief.

Classical devotional compilations — including works of salawat and litanies — use "Ya Muhammad" and "Ya Rasul Allah" as expressions of love and longing, within the framework of addressing Allah as the ultimate source of all power.
