## The Short Answer

Yes, for the vast majority of Muslims. The overwhelming consensus of Sunni scholars is that a Muslim who has not reached the rank of an independent jurist (*mujtahid*) should follow one of the four established schools of Islamic law: Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, or Hanbali.

This is not "blind following" — it is the responsible approach to religious practice.

## Why?

Everyone agrees that the Quran and Sunnah are the ultimate sources of Islamic law. The question is *how* to derive specific rulings from them. This requires:

- Mastery of classical Arabic grammar, morphology, and rhetoric
- Knowledge of tens of thousands of hadiths and their chains of narration
- Understanding of which Quranic verses and hadiths abrogate others
- Familiarity with the principles of jurisprudence (*usul al-fiqh*)
- Knowledge of scholarly consensus (*ijma'*) across the generations

The four madhabs represent centuries of rigorous scholarship applying these tools. When you follow a madhab, you're not ignoring the Quran and Sunnah — you're following the methodology of scholars who dedicated their entire lives to understanding them.

The analogy is straightforward: when you're ill, you consult a doctor. You don't refuse treatment until you've attended medical school. Following qualified scholars in matters of religious law is the same principle.

## The Scholarly Consensus

For over a thousand years, the overwhelming majority of Sunni Muslims — including the greatest scholars of hadith, Quran commentary, and theology — followed one of the four madhabs. Ibn Khaldun, the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ, and every subsequent generation either performed independent ijtihad (because they were qualified) or followed qualified scholars.
