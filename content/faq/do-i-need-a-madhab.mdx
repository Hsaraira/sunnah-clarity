## The Short Answer

{/* CONTENT PLACEHOLDER — Structural scaffolding. Actual content to be written by a qualified human author. */}

Yes, for the vast majority of Muslims. The overwhelming consensus of Sunni scholars is that a Muslim who has not reached the rank of an independent jurist (*mujtahid*) should follow one of the four established schools of Islamic law (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, or Hanbali).

This is not "blind following" — it is the responsible approach to religious practice. Just as you follow a doctor's medical advice without having attended medical school, you follow qualified scholars who have devoted their lives to mastering Islamic sciences.

## Why Not Just "Follow the Quran and Sunnah"?

Everyone agrees that the Quran and Sunnah are the ultimate sources. The question is *how* to derive rulings from them. This requires:

- Mastery of classical Arabic
- Knowledge of thousands of hadiths and their authentication
- Understanding of abrogation (*naskh*)
- Familiarity with the principles of jurisprudence (*usul al-fiqh*)
- Knowledge of scholarly consensus (*ijma'*)

The four madhabs represent centuries of rigorous scholarship applying these tools.
