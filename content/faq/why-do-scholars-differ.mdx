## The Short Answer

Scholars differ because the process of extracting specific rulings from the Quran and Sunnah inherently involves interpretation — and qualified scholars can arrive at different valid conclusions from the same evidence. This is a feature of Islamic law, not a flaw. The Prophet ﷺ himself approved of his Companions arriving at different conclusions through their reasoning.

## Why Differences Occur

**1. Different hadiths reached different scholars.** In the early centuries, hadith collections hadn't been compiled yet. A scholar in Medina might have access to narrations that a scholar in Kufa did not, and vice versa.

**2. Different assessments of hadith authenticity.** Scholars sometimes disagreed on whether a particular narrator was reliable, leading to different conclusions about which hadiths could serve as legal evidence.

**3. Different interpretations of the same text.** Arabic words can carry multiple valid meanings. The Quran says to wipe "your heads" in wudu — does this mean the entire head or part of it? Both interpretations are linguistically valid.

**4. Different methodological principles.** The Hanafi school gives significant weight to analogical reasoning (*qiyas*), while the Hanbali school prefers to rely on hadith texts even if they are weak. Neither approach is wrong — they are different valid methodologies.

Classical manuals such as [Ibn Rushd's](/scholars/ibn-rushd) *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* walk through how the four schools apply these principles to the same evidence and why multiple sound conclusions can result.

## Is This a Problem?

No. Scholarly disagreement on secondary matters is normal, expected, and even beneficial. It provides flexibility for different contexts and communities. The scholars agreed on all fundamental matters — the five pillars, the six articles of faith, the major prohibitions — and differed only on details of practice.

As scholars have noted, the disagreement of the scholars is a mercy for the community. It means there is usually more than one valid way to practice.
