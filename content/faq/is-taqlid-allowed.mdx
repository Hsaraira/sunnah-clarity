## The Short Answer

Yes. *Taqlid* — following a qualified scholar's ruling without independently verifying the evidence — is not only allowed but *necessary* for anyone who has not achieved the rank of a mujtahid (independent jurist). The Quran itself commands it: "Ask the people of knowledge if you do not know" (16:43).

## What Taqlid Actually Is

Taqlid is often mistranslated as "blind following," giving it a negative connotation. In reality, it is the normal, sensible practice of relying on expertise you don't personally possess. You follow a doctor's diagnosis without repeating medical school. You rely on an engineer's calculations without studying structural engineering. Islamic law is a rigorous science — following its qualified practitioners is rational, not blind.

## What the Scholars Say

Imam al-Nawawi explains that the scholarly consensus (*ijma'*) is that a layperson is obligated to follow a qualified scholar in matters of Islamic law. This has been the practice of Muslims since the time of the Companions — ordinary Muslims asked the scholars among them, and the scholars gave answers based on their knowledge.

The alternative — every individual Muslim independently evaluating hadith evidence — is both impractical and dangerous. It requires mastery of classical Arabic, knowledge of tens of thousands of hadiths, understanding of abrogation, and familiarity with juristic principles that take decades to acquire.
