## The Short Answer

Yes. Collective dhikr (gathering to remember Allah together) is not only permissible — it is explicitly praised in multiple authentic hadiths from both Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, the two most authenticated hadith collections in existence.

## The Direct Evidence

The Prophet ﷺ said: "No people gather to remember Allah but the angels surround them, mercy covers them, tranquility descends upon them, and Allah mentions them to those who are with Him" (Sahih Muslim, no. 2700).

In Sahih al-Bukhari (no. 6408), the Prophet ﷺ describes how Allah has angels who roam the roads seeking out gatherings of dhikr. When they find people remembering Allah, the angels call out to each other: "Come to what you are looking for!" and they enfold the gathering with their wings.

These hadiths directly and unambiguously describe *groups* of people gathering for remembrance of Allah — and describe extraordinary divine blessings attending such gatherings.

## The Scholarly Position

[Imam al-Nawawi](/scholars/imam-al-nawawi), the greatest commentator on Sahih Muslim, dedicates entire sections of his works to collective dhikr and its merits. Scholars across all four madhabs — Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali — have affirmed the permissibility and virtue of gathering for the remembrance of Allah.

The claim that group dhikr is forbidden directly contradicts the two most authentic hadith collections.
