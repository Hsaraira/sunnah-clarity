## The Short Answer

Yes. Collective dhikr (gathering to remember Allah together) is not only permissible — it is explicitly praised in multiple authentic hadiths from Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. The claim that group dhikr is an innovation directly contradicts the two most authenticated hadith collections in existence.

## The Direct Prophetic Evidence

### Hadith 1: Angels Surround the Gatherings

<HadithBlock
  arabic="مَا اجْتَمَعَ قَوْمٌ يَذْكُرُونَ اللهَ إِلَّا حَفَّتْهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ وَغَشِيَتْهُمُ الرَّحْمَةُ وَنَزَلَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ السَّكِينَةُ وَذَكَرَهُمُ اللهُ فِيمَنْ عِنْدَهُ"
  translation="No people gather to remember Allah but the angels surround them, mercy covers them, tranquility descends upon them, and Allah mentions them to those who are with Him."
  source="Sahih Muslim, no. 2700"
  narrator="Abu Hurayra (رضي الله عنه)"
/>

Notice the phrase "no people gather" (*ma ijtama'a qawm*) — this is explicitly describing a *group* activity. The hadith mentions four rewards: angelic presence, mercy, tranquility, and being mentioned by Allah to the angels. This is about collective remembrance, not individual.

### Hadith 2: Allah's Angels Seek Out Dhikr Gatherings

<HadithBlock
  arabic="إِنَّ لِلَّهِ مَلَائِكَةً يَطُوفُونَ فِي الطُّرُقِ يَلْتَمِسُونَ أَهْلَ الذِّكْرِ فَإِذَا وَجَدُوا قَوْمًا يَذْكُرُونَ اللهَ تَنَادَوْا هَلُمُّوا إِلَى حَاجَتِكُمْ"
  translation="Allah has angels who roam the roads seeking out the people of dhikr. When they find a group remembering Allah, they call out to each other: 'Come to what you are looking for!' and they enfold them with their wings up to the lowest heaven."
  source="Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 6408; Sahih Muslim, no. 2689"
  narrator="Abu Hurayra (رضي الله عنه)"
/>

The full hadith continues: Allah then asks the angels (though He already knows), "What are My servants saying?" They reply: "They are glorifying You, praising You, and declaring Your greatness." Allah says: "I bear witness that I have forgiven them." One angel says: "Among them is so-and-so who is not one of them; he only came for a personal need." Allah replies: **"They are the people whose companion will not suffer."**

This hadith describes a *scene* — a group sitting together doing dhikr, angels physically flocking to them, and Allah forgiving the entire gathering, including someone who merely sat with them.

### Hadith 3: "Shall I Not Tell You the Best of Your Deeds?"

<HadithBlock
  arabic="أَلَا أُنَبِّئُكُمْ بِخَيْرِ أَعْمَالِكُمْ وَأَزْكَاهَا عِنْدَ مَلِيكِكُمْ وَأَرْفَعِهَا فِي دَرَجَاتِكُمْ وَخَيْرٌ لَكُمْ مِنْ إِنْفَاقِ الذَّهَبِ وَالْوَرِقِ"
  translation="Shall I not tell you of the best of your deeds, the purest of them in the sight of your Sovereign, the most exalted of them in your ranks, better for you than spending gold and silver, and better for you than meeting your enemy and striking their necks? They said: 'Yes, O Messenger of Allah.' He said: 'The remembrance of Allah.'"
  source="Sunan al-Tirmidhi, no. 3377; graded sahih"
  narrator="Abu al-Darda (رضي الله عنه)"
/>

Dhikr itself is ranked *higher than jihad and charity* in this hadith. When the Prophet ﷺ describes group dhikr gatherings as surrounded by angels and covered in mercy (in the hadiths above), and then ranks dhikr as the best of deeds, the combined evidence for collective remembrance is overwhelming.

## The Companion Practice

The Companions practiced group dhikr during the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime:

<HadithBlock
  translation="A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: 'The messengers of Allah (angels) entered upon you.' The Prophet ﷺ said: 'They are angels who came because of a gathering that was in another part of Medina, remembering Allah. Had you been with them, you would have been counted with them.'"
  source="Sahih Muslim, derived from the hadith of the angels seeking dhikr gatherings"
/>

And after the Prophet ﷺ:

<HadithBlock
  translation="Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنهما) said: 'Dhikr in a group after the prayer used to be the practice in the time of the Prophet ﷺ.' Ibn Abbas added: 'I used to know when they had finished by hearing the dhikr.'"
  source="Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 841; Sahih Muslim, no. 583"
  narrator="Ibn Abbas (رضي الله عنهما)"
/>

This proves audible, group dhikr after prayer was standard practice during the Prophet's lifetime — loud enough that Ibn Abbas could hear it from outside the mosque.

## What the Classical Scholars Say

<ScholarQuote
  scholar="Imam al-Nawawi"
  scholarSlug="imam-al-nawawi"
  title="Commentator on Sahih Muslim, author of al-Adhkar"
  dates="d. 676 AH / 1278 CE"
  source="al-Adhkar"
  quote="Know that dhikr is recommended for groups, based on the well-known hadiths. The evidence for this is overwhelming and well-established."
/>

Al-Nawawi dedicates entire chapters of *al-Adhkar* — his comprehensive book on remembrance — to the merits of group dhikr, citing the hadiths above and others. He does not express any reservation about it.

<MisconceptionBlock
  claim="Group dhikr is bid'a because the Prophet ﷺ only did dhikr individually."
  response={
    <>
      The hadiths in Sahih al-Bukhari (no. 6408) and Sahih Muslim (no. 2700) explicitly describe groups of people sitting together doing dhikr, with the Prophet ﷺ praising this practice and describing extraordinary divine rewards for it. Saying the Prophet ﷺ &quot;only did dhikr individually&quot; requires ignoring the two most authenticated books in Sunni Islam.
    </>
  }
/>
