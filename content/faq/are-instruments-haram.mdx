## The Short Answer

The relied-upon position across the four Sunni schools is that musical instruments — other than the *duff* (frame drum) — are impermissible. However, a documented classical minority permitted limited forms under strict conditions. SeekersGuidance advises following the majority position out of caution while warning against condemning others in a genuine area of scholarly disagreement.

## The Majority View

The four schools treat stringed instruments (like the *'ud*/lute), wind instruments, and most percussion beyond the duff as impermissible. A commonly cited hadith is in Sahih al-Bukhari (no. 5590), where the Prophet ﷺ mentions people who will seek to make *ma'azif* (musical instruments) lawful.

## The Minority View

[Imam al-Ghazali](/scholars/imam-al-ghazali) dedicated a full chapter of *Ihya Ulum al-Din* to *sama'* (spiritual audition), arguing that the ruling depends on the listener's state and context, not on the instrument alone. Some Shafi'i and Maliki scholars took similar positions under conditions. This opinion is historical, documented, and not a modern invention — but it remains a minority.

## SeekersGuidance's Practical Counsel

SeekersGuidance typically urges caution and discourages instrument-heavy "Islamic music" culture, while explicitly warning against condemning Muslims who follow a legitimate scholarly opinion. The principle: "one should not condemn others" in areas of real juristic difference.
