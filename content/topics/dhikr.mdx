## What Is Dhikr?

{/* CONTENT PLACEHOLDER — Structural scaffolding. Actual content to be written by a qualified human author. */}

*Dhikr* (ذكر) literally means "remembrance" or "mention." In Islamic practice, it refers to the remembrance of Allah through specific phrases, supplications, and litanies. Allah commands dhikr repeatedly in the Quran:

> "Remember Me, and I will remember you." — Quran 2:152

## Individual and Collective Dhikr

Both individual and collective forms of dhikr are well-established in the prophetic tradition and the practice of the Companions.

<MisconceptionBlock
  claim="Group dhikr is a forbidden innovation with no basis in the Sunnah."
  response="Numerous authentic hadiths describe gatherings of remembrance. In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ said: 'No people gather to remember Allah but the angels surround them, mercy covers them, tranquility descends upon them, and Allah mentions them to those who are with Him.' Imam al-Nawawi compiled an entire book (al-Adhkar) documenting the prophetic practice of dhikr in all its forms."

## The Prophetic Practice

The Prophet ﷺ engaged in dhikr constantly and taught his Companions specific formulas for every occasion — upon waking, before sleeping, after prayers, during travel, and in gatherings.

<ScholarQuote
  scholar="Imam al-Nawawi"
  title="al-Adhkar"
  translation="Know that the excellence of dhikr is not restricted to tasbih, tahlil, tahmid, takbir, and the like. Rather, every person who acts in obedience to Allah is engaged in the remembrance of Allah."
