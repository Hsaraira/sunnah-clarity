## What Is a Madhab?

{/* CONTENT PLACEHOLDER — Structural scaffolding. Actual content to be written by a qualified human author. */}

A *madhab* (مذهب) is a school of Islamic jurisprudence — a comprehensive methodology for deriving legal rulings from the Quran and Sunnah. The four surviving Sunni schools are:

1. **Hanafi** — founded by Imam Abu Hanifa (d. 150 AH)
2. **Maliki** — founded by Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH)
3. **Shafi'i** — founded by Imam al-Shafi'i (d. 204 AH)
4. **Hanbali** — founded by Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 241 AH)

## Why Follow a Madhab?

  claim="Following a madhab is blind following (taqlid) — Muslims should go directly to the Quran and Sunnah."

## The Scholarly Consensus on Madhabs

For over a thousand years, the vast majority of Sunni Muslims — including the greatest scholars of hadith, tafsir, and aqida — followed one of the four madhabs. This was not a deviation; it was the normative practice of the ummah.

<ScholarQuote
  scholar="Shah Waliullah al-Dihlawi"
