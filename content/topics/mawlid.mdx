## What Is the Mawlid?

{/* CONTENT PLACEHOLDER — Structural scaffolding for the MDX pipeline. Actual theological content will be written by a qualified human author citing primary Islamic sources. */}

The *mawlid* (مولد) refers to the commemoration of the birth of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, traditionally observed on the 12th of Rabi' al-Awwal. It is a practice that has been observed by the majority of the Muslim world for over eight centuries.

## The Scholarly Position

The permissibility — and indeed the recommendation — of commemorating the Prophet's ﷺ birth has been affirmed by scholars from all four Sunni schools of law. Among the most notable:

- **Imam al-Suyuti** (d. 911 AH) wrote an entire treatise in its defense
- **Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani** (d. 852 AH) considered it a praiseworthy innovation
- **Imam al-Nawawi's** principles on categorizing bid'a support its permissibility

## The Evidence

The Prophet ﷺ himself marked the day of his birth. When asked about fasting on Mondays, he replied:

> "That is the day on which I was born, and the day on which I received revelation." — Sahih Muslim
