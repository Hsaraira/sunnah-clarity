## What Is Bid'a?

{/* CONTENT PLACEHOLDER — This section will be written by a qualified human author citing primary Islamic sources. The text below is structural scaffolding to verify the MDX pipeline. */}

The Arabic word *bid'a* (بدعة) literally means "something new" or "an innovation." In Islamic scholarship, its meaning is far more nuanced than the blanket condemnation often presented in popular discourse.

## The Five Categories of Innovation

Classical scholars, most notably **al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam** (d. 660 AH) and **Imam al-Nawawi** (d. 676 AH), categorized innovations according to the five rulings of Islamic law:

1. **Obligatory** (*wajiba*) — innovations necessary to preserve the religion
2. **Recommended** (*manduba*) — praiseworthy innovations like writing books of knowledge
3. **Permissible** (*mubaha*) — neutral innovations in worldly matters
4. **Disliked** (*makruha*) — innovations that are better avoided
5. **Prohibited** (*muharrama*) — innovations that contradict established principles

## The Hadith "Every Innovation Is Misguidance"

<MisconceptionBlock
  claim="The Prophet ﷺ said 'every innovation is misguidance,' so ALL innovations — including mawlid, group dhikr, and tarawih in congregation — are forbidden."
  response="This hadith is interpreted by the vast majority of classical scholars as referring to innovations that contradict established principles of Islam, not every new practice. Imam al-Nawawi, who is the primary commentator of Sahih Muslim itself, explicitly states that bid'a is divided into categories. The general expression ('every') is qualified by the specific evidence and scholarly consensus."

## The Evidence From the Companions

The Companions themselves introduced practices that did not exist during the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime:

- **Umar ibn al-Khattab** (رضي الله عنه) gathered people for Tarawih prayer behind one imam, calling it "an excellent bid'a" (*ni'mat al-bid'a*) — recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari
- **Uthman ibn Affan** (رضي الله عنه) added the second adhan for Jumu'ah prayer
- The compilation of the Quran into a single mushaf
- The diacritical marks (*tashkil*) added to the Quranic text
